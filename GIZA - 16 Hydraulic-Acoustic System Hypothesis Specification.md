# 1. Objective

The first scientific model implemented inside GIZA will test the hypothesis that the Great Pyramid functioned as a giant hydraulic-acoustic resonator rather than solely as a royal tomb.

The purpose of the simulation is **not to prove the hypothesis**, but to determine whether the proposed physical mechanisms are internally consistent and physically plausible when subjected to numerical simulation.

The software must therefore serve as a scientific experimentation platform capable of validating or falsifying competing hypotheses.

# 3. Central Hypothesis

The Great Pyramid is modeled as a coupled hydraulic and acoustic energy system.

The primary energy source of the hydraulic-acoustic system is hydrostatic pressure generated by the Osiris Shaft.

The hypothesis proposes that the Osiris Shaft functioned as a deep vertical pressure reservoir. A dedicated underground water conduit continuously connected the shaft to the hydraulic network beneath the Great Pyramid.

The water column contained within the Osiris Shaft generated hydrostatic pressure proportional to its height. This pressure was transmitted through the conduit into the subterranean hydraulic system, where it could drive oscillatory water flow, pressure pulses, and acoustic excitation.

The proposed energy pathway is therefore:

Water Supply
      │
      ▼
Osiris Shaft
(Vertical Water Column)
      │
Hydrostatic Pressure
      │
Underground Water Conduit
      │
Subterranean Chamber
      │
Hydraulic Oscillations
      │
Ascending Passage
      │
Grand Gallery
      │
King's Chamber
      │
Granite Resonance

Unlike models requiring mechanical pumps, this hypothesis assumes that gravitational potential energy stored in the elevated water column of the Osiris Shaft is the primary driver of the hydraulic system.

Within this hypothesis, the Osiris Shaft is not merely a well or ceremonial structure.

It is modeled as the primary hydraulic pressure reservoir of the entire pyramid complex.

Maintaining a stable vertical water column.

# 16. Success Criteria

The hypothesis is considered **worthy of further investigation** if simulations demonstrate one or more of the following:

* Stable hydraulic oscillations can develop within the measured geometry.
* Resonance frequencies emerge naturally without requiring unrealistic parameter values.
* Acoustic amplification occurs in chambers consistent with the proposed mechanism.
* Coupled hydraulic-acoustic interactions produce coherent energy transfer.
* Predicted behavior is robust across realistic environmental conditions.

The hypothesis is considered **falsified or weakened** if simulations require physically implausible assumptions, fail to produce the predicted phenomena, or are highly unstable under realistic boundary conditions.

---

# 17. Guiding Principle

The GIZA platform is not designed to advocate any particular explanation for the Great Pyramid. Its role is to provide a rigorous, physics-based simulation environment in which competing hypotheses can be evaluated using identical archaeological data and transparent computational methods.

The hydraulic-acoustic hypothesis is selected as the first implementation because it involves the strongest coupling between geometry, fluid dynamics, acoustics, and structural mechanics, making it an ideal test case for validating the platform's scientific capabilities.
