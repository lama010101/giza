# Great Pyramid of Giza — Measurement Reference

## Sources

Primary survey sources:

> Petrie, W.M.F. *The Pyramids and Temples of Gizeh.* London: Field & Tuer, 1883. Measurements in British inches, probable error ±0.06" for first-class points.

> Cole, J.H. "Determination of the Exact Size and Orientation of the Great Pyramid of Giza." Survey of Egypt, 1925. Measurements in meters, accuracy ~1:500,000 using invar wires.

> Dash, G. & Paulson, J. "The 2015 Survey of the Base of the Great Pyramid." *Journal of Ancient Egyptian Architecture*, 2016. Based on Lehner-Goodman 1984 GPMP data.

> Gantenbrink, R. "The Upuaut Project." 1993. Robot survey of King's and Queen's Chamber shafts.

> Howard Vyse, R.W.H. *Operations Carried on at the Pyramids of Gizeh in 1837.* London, 1841. Relieving chamber dimensions.

> Dash, G. "Where in the World Is the Great Pyramid?" 2018. GPS survey of summit station.

Supplementary:

> Lehner, M. & Goodman, D. Giza Plateau Mapping Project (GPMP), 1984. AERA.

> Legon, J.A.R. "A Ground Plan at Giza." *Discussions in Egyptology* 10, 1988.

## Notes

1. Petrie's measurements are in British inches (1 in = 25.4 mm). Conversions to meters use 1 in = 0.0254 m.
2. The royal cubit value of 0.52375 m (20.620 in) was derived by Petrie primarily from the King's Chamber dimensions.
3. Cole 1925 is the most precise base survey, using invar wires with accuracy ~1:500,000.
4. Dash 2015 reanalyzed the 1984 Lehner-Goodman GPMP data using linear regression, yielding mean side 230.329 m.
5. The three base surveys (Petrie, Cole, Dash) agree within ~3.5 cm — remarkable for a 230 m structure.
6. Gantenbrink's 1993 Upuaut Project provides the only robot-surveyed measurements of the shaft interiors.
7. Howard-Vyse's 1837 relieving chamber measurements are in feet/inches and less precise than Petrie's work.
8. The Subterranean Chamber is very roughly hewn and unfinished — dimensions are approximate and highly irregular.
9. The Well Shaft and Grotto are irregular; published measurements are approximate.
10. No complete laser scan or photogrammetric survey of the interior has been published as of 2025. The ScanPyramids mission used muon radiography, not optical scanning.
11. No publicly released full-resolution LiDAR or complete photogrammetric model of the pyramid interior exists as of 2026.
12. The "Big Void" detected by ScanPyramids (2017) is not yet confirmed by direct observation.
13. GPS coordinates: the Dash 2018 survey established WGS84 coordinates for the first time; earlier coordinates were based on the Helmert ellipsoid and were ~180 m off.
