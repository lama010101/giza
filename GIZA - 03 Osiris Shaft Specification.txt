
# 1. Osiris Shaft Reconstruction — Introduction

## Purpose

The Osiris Shaft serves as the first fully explorable environment and establishes the project's reconstruction methodology.

It will be the reference implementation for:

* evidence integration
* geometry reconstruction
* camera systems
* interaction
* scientific annotation
* performance optimization

Subsequent environments (e.g., the Great Pyramid) should follow the same architectural conventions.

## Scope

The reconstruction will include:

* Surface entrance and surrounding plateau context.
* Vertical shaft and descent path.
* All three documented subterranean levels.
* Water-filled lower chamber.
* Central island and sarcophagus.
* Northern conduit and accessible tunnel segments.
* Modern exploration infrastructure (where present), clearly distinguished from ancient features.
* Evidence hotspots linked to sources and confidence scores.

The next section ("20. Osiris Shaft — Historical & Archaeological Specification") will begin the detailed reconstruction, covering chronology, dimensions, room-by-room geometry, material definitions, and evidence mapping before progressing to cameras, interactions, and implementation details.

# 2. Osiris Shaft — Historical & Archaeological Specification

---

# 2.1 Purpose of the Reconstruction

The objective is to reproduce the Osiris Shaft as faithfully as possible from documented archaeological evidence while clearly identifying where interpretation begins.

The reconstruction is **not** intended to depict a specific historical period as absolute fact. Instead, it represents the site's best-supported physical state, with the ability to switch between multiple chronological phases as future versions are developed.

The initial MVP reconstructs the shaft as an archaeological site rather than as an imagined functioning installation.

---

# 2.2 Geographic Location

The Osiris Shaft is located on the Giza Plateau, approximately south of the Great Pyramid, near the causeway traditionally associated with Khafre.

The application should position the shaft within a geographically accurate terrain model of the plateau using a common survey coordinate system.

Future versions will align the site with published topographic and laser survey datasets.

# 2.8 Level 0 — Surface

The reconstruction begins above ground.

Visible elements include:

* Limestone bedrock
* Desert surface
* Excavation perimeter
* Entrance opening
* Modern protective fencing (optional)
* Visitor paths
* Terrain undulations
* Reference markers

The purpose is to provide orientation before descent.

---

# 2.9 Level 1 Overview

Level 1 serves primarily as the transition between the surface and deeper chambers.

Characteristics:

* Narrow vertical shaft
* Rough limestone
* Modern access infrastructure
* Limited natural light
* Increasing humidity

Visual goals:

* Immediate perception of depth
* Scale awareness
* Spatial confinement

---

# 2.10 Level 2 Overview

Level 2 introduces larger excavated spaces.

Primary objectives:

* Demonstrate excavation geometry.
* Show relationship between vertical and horizontal circulation.
* Introduce evidence interaction.

Environmental changes:

* Lower temperature
* Less ambient light
* Higher humidity
* More sediment

---

# 2.11 Level 3 Overview

Level 3 is the centerpiece of the Osiris Shaft reconstruction.

Principal elements:

* Flooded chamber
* Central island
* Sarcophagus
* Limestone walls
* Water reflections
* Northern conduit entrance

The room should immediately communicate both archaeological significance and geological context without relying on narration.

---

# 2.12 Water System

Water is modeled as an environmental feature, not evidence of a hydraulic function.

Simulation parameters:

| Parameter          | Initial Value |
| ------------------ | ------------: |
| Surface elevation  |  Configurable |
| Transparency       |    Adjustable |
| Turbidity          |    Adjustable |
| Reflection quality |       Dynamic |
| Refraction         |       Enabled |
| Ripple simulation  |       Enabled |

Future hypothesis plugins may simulate active hydraulic behavior via the Hydraulic simulation module. See *GIZA - 11 Hypothesis Framework* §9. The default state represents observed standing water.

## Multi-Hypothesis Object

The sarcophagus is central to several competing hypotheses. The geometry, dimensions, position, material, and orientation are identical across all hypotheses. Only the interpretation changes.

```
Hypothesis                  Confidence
Mainstream Funerary         92%
Hydraulic Reservoir         73%
Acoustic Resonator          51%
Chemical Reactor            38%
Mathematical Reference      44%
Astronomical Instrument     29%
Unknown Function            10%
```

Each hypothesis provides its own overlays, annotations, and linked simulations. See *GIZA - 11 Hypothesis Framework* §2.2 for the full example.

# 2.15 Northern Conduit

The conduit is treated with exceptional care because it is central to several competing interpretations.

Default presentation:

* Observed geometry only
* Measured dimensions
* Accessible extent
* Survey references
* No assumed continuation

Optional overlays:

* Known surveyed path
* Geological projection
* Hydraulic hypothesis
* Other interpretive frameworks

The application must visually distinguish between surveyed space and inferred continuation. See *GIZA - 11 Hypothesis Framework* §12 for terminology.

---

# 2.16 Geological Context

The shaft is embedded within layered limestone.

Geological visualization modes include:

1. Photorealistic rock
2. Simplified stratigraphy
3. Cutaway geology
4. Semi-transparent terrain
5. Cross-sectional engineering view

These modes share the same underlying geometry.

---

# 2.17 Environmental Rendering

The environment should communicate age and underground conditions through physically based rendering rather than exaggerated visual effects.

Features include:

* Moisture gradients
* Mineral staining
* Calcite deposits (where documented)
* Surface roughness variation
* Soft dust accumulation
* Subtle airborne particles
* Localized dripping effects

No decorative "fantasy cave" elements are permitted.

---

# 2.18 Lighting Strategy

Lighting follows three operational modes:

### Documentary Mode

Balanced illumination approximating professional archaeological photography.

### Exploration Mode

Low ambient illumination with realistic darkness, supplemented by the user's virtual light source where appropriate.

### Scientific Inspection Mode

Even, neutral lighting that minimizes shadows to reveal geometry and surface details.

Lighting parameters should be physically plausible and configurable without altering the underlying material properties.
