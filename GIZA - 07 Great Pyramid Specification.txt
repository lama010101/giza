
# 1. Great Pyramid Reconstruction — Introduction

## Purpose

The Great Pyramid of Khufu is the second fully explorable environment in the GIZA platform and the first monument-scale reconstruction.

It extends the methodology established by the Osiris Shaft specification (see *GIZA - Osiris Shaft Specification*) to a far larger, more complex, and more contested structure.

It serves as the reference implementation for:

* multi-chamber monuments
* theory-independent reconstruction at scale
* chronology layer switching
* shaft and passage systems
* relieving architecture
* high-density evidence hotspots
* large-mesh streaming and LOD management

Subsequent monuments (Khafre, Menkaure, satellite pyramids) should follow the same architectural conventions.

## Scope

The reconstruction will include:

* Surrounding plateau context and pyramid enclosure.
* Casing fragments and reconstructed casing surface.
* Core masonry mass.
* Original entrance and modern entrance tunnel (Al-Mamun).
* Descending Passage.
* Subterranean Chamber and its subsidiaries.
* Ascending Passage and plug blocks.
* Grand Gallery.
* King's Chamber, Antechamber, and relieving chambers.
* Queen's Chamber and its shafts.
* King's Chamber shafts (including the northern and southern "air shafts").
* Well Shaft (Service Shaft) and the Grotto.
* Modern exploration infrastructure (clearly distinguished from ancient features).
* Evidence hotspots linked to sources and confidence scores.
* Chronology layers from Old Kingdom construction to modern excavation.

The next section ("2. Great Pyramid — Historical & Archaeological Specification") provides the detailed reconstruction, covering chronology, geometry, chamber-by-chamber specification, materials, evidence mapping, interaction, and implementation considerations.

# 2. Great Pyramid — Historical & Archaeological Specification

---

# 2.1 Purpose of the Reconstruction

The objective is to reproduce the Great Pyramid as faithfully as possible from documented survey and archaeological evidence while clearly identifying where interpretation begins.

The reconstruction is **not** intended to advocate a single function for the monument. Instead, it represents the structure's best-supported physical state, with the ability to switch between multiple chronological phases and multiple competing interpretations.

The initial MVP reconstructs the pyramid as an archaeological monument rather than as an imagined functioning installation.

---

# 2.2 Geographic Location

The Great Pyramid is located on the Giza Plateau, on the northern edge of the necropolis, at the head of the Nile floodplain.

The application should position the pyramid within the plateau terrain model using the Local Plateau Coordinate system defined in *GIZA - Technical Architecture* §4.

The pyramid's center is used as a monument-local origin. All internal chambers are positioned relative to this origin.

Future versions will align the model with published topographic and laser survey datasets (see §2.7).

# 2.5 Reconstruction Philosophy

Every modeled object belongs to one of the five classes defined in *GIZA - Osiris Shaft Specification* §2.5.

## Original Architecture

Ancient masonry, passages, chambers, and shafts.

Cannot be moved.

## Modern Installations

Modern entrance tunnel.

Wooden walkways.

Metal ladders and railings.

Lighting.

Ventilation machinery.

Clearly distinguishable.

## Environmental Objects

Dust.

Humidity.

Airflow (where measured).

Bat guano and organic deposits (where documented).

## Archaeological Finds

Casing fragments.

Plug blocks (where observed in situ or relocated).

Relieving chamber debris.

Sarcophagus (King's Chamber).

## Interpretive Objects

Only visible when a theory requires them.

Examples:

* Granite plug blocks in transit
* Counterweight assemblies (Grand Gallery)
* Portcullis systems (Antechamber)
* Hydraulic seals
* Acoustic resonator arrays
* Internal ramp segments

These never appear in Scientific Evidence mode.

# 2.8 External Architecture

The reconstruction begins outside the monument.

Visible elements include:

* Limestone bedrock and plateau surface
* Pyramid enclosure wall traces
* Corner socket stones
* Core masonry (stepped casing)
* Remaining casing stones (lower courses, north face)
* Casing fragment debris fields
* Modern entrance tunnel mouth
* Original entrance (blocked, high on north face)
* Surrounding mastaba field context (low LOD)
* Modern visitor infrastructure (optional, toggleable)

The purpose is to provide orientation and scale before entering the interior.

---

# 2.9 Core Masonry

The core is represented as a stepped mass of locally quarried limestone blocks.

Properties:

```
Static geometry

Collision enabled

Receives shadows

Procedural weathering

Erosion gullies

Casing fragment decals
```

The core mesh is authored as a single closed volume with LOD variants. Internal chambers are carved as separate geometry rather than boolean-subtracted from the core, preserving independent streaming and editing.

---

# 2.10 Casing Stones

The original Tura limestone casing is largely missing.

Default presentation:

* Remaining casing stones (north face, lower courses) shown at measured confidence.
* Reconstructed casing surface shown as a transparent overlay with explicit confidence labeling.
* Casing angle derived from surviving casing stones and socket measurements.

Optional overlays:

* Reconstructed full casing (theory layer)
* Bent pyramid-style casing hypothesis (rejected, included for comparison only)
* Casing fragment scatter

The application must visually distinguish between surviving casing and reconstructed casing.

---

# 2.11 Pyramidion

The pyramidion is missing.

Default presentation:

* Absent.
* Optional transparent reconstruction overlay.
* Multiple candidate forms (solid gold-capped, basalt, limestone) presented as theory variants.

No pyramidion geometry appears in Scientific Evidence mode.

# 2.12 Descending Passage

The Descending Passage runs from the original entrance down to the Subterranean Chamber.

Characteristics:

* Slope approximately 26° 31′
* Cross-section approximately 1.2 m × 1.0 m
* Length approximately 96 m (to Subterranean Chamber)
* Cut through core masonry and into bedrock below
* Rougher finish in bedrock section

Visual goals:

* Immediate perception of descent
* Scale awareness
* Transition from masonry to bedrock

Evidence hotspots:

* Original entrance
* Joint between masonry and bedrock
* Mummy pit (excavation, southern side)
* Modern ventilation shaft penetration

---

# 2.13 Subterranean Chamber

The Subterranean Chamber is cut into the bedrock below the pyramid center.

Principal elements:

* Rough-hewn rectangular chamber
* Central pit (variously interpreted as unfinished sarcophagus recess or cistern)
* Southern niche
* Western, northern, and eastern blind passages (incomplete)
* Connecting horizontal passage from Descending Passage
* Rough floor and ceiling

The chamber appears unfinished in the archaeological record.

Default presentation:

* Observed geometry only
* Measured dimensions
* Visible tool marks (where documented)
* No assumed function

Optional overlays:

* Tomb hypothesis (sarcophagus recess)
* Cistern hypothesis (hydraulic)
* Abandoned primary chamber hypothesis
* Ritual chamber hypothesis

The application must visually distinguish between surveyed space and inferred features.

---

# 2.14 Ascending Passage

The Ascending Passage rises from the junction with the Descending Passage to the Grand Gallery.

Characteristics:

* Slope approximately 26° 02′
* Cross-section approximately 1.0 m × 1.2 m
* Length approximately 39 m
* Blocked by three granite plug blocks (lower end)
* Limestone masonry walls (fine Tura limestone)

Default presentation:

* Passage geometry
* Plug blocks in observed positions
* Modern bypass tunnel (Al-Mamun) clearly distinguished

* Plug block emplacement hypotheses
* Sealing sequence theories

# 2.16 King's Chamber

The King's Chamber is the highest principal chamber and the most precisely constructed.

Characteristics:

* Height approximately 5.8 m

* Floor of granite
* Flat ceiling of granite beams
* Sarcophagus (broken) along west wall

Principal elements:

* Granite walls (smoothly dressed)
* Granite floor
* Granite ceiling beams
* Sarcophagus
* Northern shaft entrance
* Southern shaft entrance
* Wall joint evidence

# 2.20 Queen's Chamber Shafts

The Queen's Chamber shafts are unusual: they do not exit the pyramid and were sealed at their inner ends until 1872.

Characteristics:

* Approximately 0.20 m × 0.20 m cross-section
* Horizontal initial section
* Upward bend
* Terminate before reaching the pyramid exterior
* Southern shaft explored by Upuaut-2 robot (Gantenbrink, 1993)
* Northern shaft explored by later robots (2002, 2010)

Default presentation:

* Observed geometry (surveyed portions)
* Sealed inner ends (as found in 1872)
* "Door" / blocking stones (Gantenbrink, Pyramid Rover)
* Uns surveyed portions marked as inferred

Optional overlays:

* Star-shaft hypothesis (Krupp / Bauval)
* Soul-shaft hypothesis
* Hydraulic conduit hypothesis
* Acoustic waveguide hypothesis

The application must visually distinguish between robot-surveyed segments and inferred continuation.

# 2.26 Environmental Rendering

The environment should communicate age, dryness, and confinement through physically based rendering rather than exaggerated visual effects.

Features include:

* Dust accumulation
* Salt efflorescence (where documented)
* Smoke blackening (where documented)
* Surface roughness variation
* Micro cracks
* Subtle airborne dust
* Localized humidity gradients (Subterranean Chamber, Well Shaft)

No decorative "fantasy tomb" elements are permitted.

---

# 2.27 Lighting Strategy

Lighting follows the three operational modes defined in *GIZA - Osiris Shaft Specification* §2.18.

### Documentary Mode

Balanced illumination approximating professional archaeological photography.

### Exploration Mode

Low ambient illumination with realistic darkness, supplemented by the user's virtual light source.

### Scientific Inspection Mode

Even, neutral lighting that minimizes shadows to reveal geometry and surface details.

Additional pyramid-specific notes:

* The Grand Gallery and King's Chamber benefit from indirect bounce lighting to reveal corbelling and granite grain.
* The Subterranean Chamber remains dark by default; ambient lift is provided only in Scientific Inspection Mode.
* Shaft interiors are not lit by ambient sources; users must enable inspection lights or robot-mounted lights.

Lighting parameters should be physically plausible and configurable without altering the underlying material properties.

A pyramid-specific interaction.

# 2.31 Theory-Independent Reconstruction

The Great Pyramid is the canonical example of theory-independent reconstruction.

Geometry exists independently of interpretation.

```
Geometry

+

Evidence

+

Theory
```

Example:

```
King's Chamber

Geometry
    identical

Theory A
    Royal burial chamber

Theory B
    Acoustic resonator

Theory C
    Hydraulic pressure vessel

Theory D
    Initiation chamber

Theory E
    Energy generator
```

Only the explanation changes.

The geometry does not.

Each theory is presented with:

```
Supporting Evidence

Contradictory Evidence

Unknowns

Engineering Simulation (if any)

Bibliography
```

Theory schema is defined in *GIZA - Data Architecture* §-13.

# 2.33 Performance Considerations

The Great Pyramid is significantly larger than the Osiris Shaft and requires stricter streaming discipline.

### Scene Streaming

Initial load:

```
Plateau context (low LOD)

Pyramid exterior (LOD2)

Modern entrance

UI
```

After entering:

```
Descending Passage

Subterranean Chamber
```

Later:

```
Ascending Passage

Grand Gallery
```

Finally:

```
King's Chamber

Relieving Chambers

Queen's Chamber

Shafts (on demand)
```

Assets unload when sufficiently distant unless pinned by the user.

### Mesh Budgets

The pyramid exterior and core dominate the triangle budget.

| Component                  | LOD0 Triangles | LOD2 Triangles |
| -------------------------- | -------------: | -------------: |
| Exterior + core            |      2,500,000 |        400,000 |
| Grand Gallery              |        400,000 |         60,000 |
| King's Chamber             |        300,000 |         50,000 |
| Relieving Chambers         |        200,000 |         30,000 |
| Queen's Chamber            |        200,000 |         40,000 |
| Subterranean Chamber       |        250,000 |         50,000 |
| Shafts (per shaft)         |        120,000 |         20,000 |
| Casing reconstruction      |        150,000 |         25,000 |

These budgets are illustrative; final values are governed by *GIZA - Technical Architecture* §6.27 and *GIZA - Asset Production Pipeline*.

### Cross-Section Performance

Cross-section clipping planes (see *GIZA - Technical Architecture* §7.18) must operate on the entire pyramid volume without generating duplicate geometry.

Cut surfaces display geological texture or grid overlay.

### Shaft Rendering

Shafts are narrow and prone to clipping artifacts.

Mitigations:

* Reduced near-clip distance during shaft traversal
* Dedicated shaft camera presets
* Capped LOD for shaft interiors
* Optional wireframe mode for uns surveyed segments

### Memory Management

Only one principal chamber should remain fully resident in GPU memory at a time, unless the user pins additional chambers.

The Well Shaft and Grotto stream on demand.

Relieving chambers stream only when accessed via virtual flythrough.

---

# 2.34 Proposed Enhancement: Multi-Monument Cross-Section

Future versions should permit a plateau-scale cross-section plane that simultaneously cuts the Great Pyramid, Khafre, Menkaure, and the Osiris Shaft.

This requires a shared Local Plateau Coordinate system and consistent LOD discipline across monuments.

The cut plane must not generate duplicate geometry and must respect per-monument evidence confidence.
